## LCS a brief history
My first introduction to LCS was at the AX Technical Conference in Redmond way back in 2013.  I was one of the few customers attending the Tech Conference back then and I remember distinctly the introduction sessions.  Many partners were livid that Microsoft was introducing this toolset that they saw as a threat to their revenue model.   As a customer I was thrilled at all of these new and great capabilities that I would have access to,  without needing a partner.   

Unfortunately it was not until LCS matured years later with D365 and full cloud hosting that the vision was realized.   Even then the tool remained incomplete and at times frustrating to use.   My first instruction to new admins learning LCS was that step 2 of any process is 'now wait'.   It is with this lens that we look forward to PPAC.   Not with rose coloured glasses but with an honest appraisal of what LCS is,  and what it is not. 

## Enter PPAC
Prior to starting to use PPAC to manage D365 F&O environments my impression of the tool was and continues to be less than stellar.   My previous experience has been trying to use the tool to manage Power Platform environments for purpose of wrangling citizen developer applications.   A task which the tools were seemingly not designed to do.  

So it is with some trepidation that I embark on this journey to adjust my workflows and mindset from LCS to PPAC.
